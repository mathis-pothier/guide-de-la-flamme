Welcome to Enshrouded 101, where we'll go over 10 tips for new and beginning players. Whether it's about building your first base, picking the right skills, or venturing into the Hollow Halls, we're going to cover everything you need to know. And by the end of this video, I guarantee you'll come out the other side better equipped and ready for your adventures in Embervale. When you start in a new world, you'll have the option to pick your difficulty and change your settings. If this is your first run, I recommend sticking to the default experience. If you want something chill, you can lower things like enemy health and damage, or speed up crafting and XP. And if you prefer a more unforgiving run, you can crank the enemies up or even turn on starvation. You can also change these settings later in game, so you don't have to worry about having the right settings at the start. Eventually, you'll have your own custom settings you prefer, which is the boat I'm in, but try to play the game as intended your first time, then tweak things as you go. Tip two is about the Flame Altar, one of the first things you'll be introduced to in your main quest. After completing the tutorial, you're going to be asked to place your Flame Altar. And this is essentially the heart of your base. Your Altar marks out the area you'll be able to build in, so you can plan your base around it. Every Altar you place also has a fast travel point. So, as you set up more bases around the map, getting to and from these places becomes a whole lot easier. Eventually, you can have up to 16 of these guys, so don't be afraid to use them. If you ever go on expeditions, you can always bring a Flame Altar with you and use it as a temporary fast travel point. But most importantly, upgrading your Flame Altar is the main progression in Enshrouded. Each upgrade will help you survive better in the Shroud, opening up parts of the map that were once blocked off before. So, if you ever feel stuck, check and see if your Altar needs upgrades. Tip three is about building your first base. It doesn't need to be anything fancy. At the start, it's really just a place to put your workstation, so you're not crafting out in the open. Now, one thing I always recommend is picking up everything. A lot of building blocks and recipes in Enshrouded unlock just by picking up new materials for the first time. So, if you see something that you haven't grabbed before, be sure to pick it up. For building, you'll need a crafting hammer, which you can make at the workbench. Once you equip it, you'll have a building mode for placing walls, floors, roofs, as well as a deconstruction mode for picking pieces back up. Another cool thing is that when you deconstruct a piece, you'll get all of your resources back, so don't stress about mistakes. Don't be afraid to build things, move things, or just try something else entirely. You'll also want to build the workbench early. This is where you're going to be crafting your building blocks along with a lot of your early tools and gear like the grappling hook and the glider. As you keep exploring and leveling up, you'll keep unlocking more types of blocks and shapes, [music] so your builds will naturally get better over time. Also, your base isn't just for work stations because there is another big reason you'll need one, which leads to tip four, rescue as many survivors as you could find. Out in the world, you'll find villagers that succumb to the shroud or by other means, and once you find them, you'll be able to rescue them and summon them back to your base. Survivors are going to be how you unlock most of your crafting stations and recipes, the blacksmith, the hunter, the alchemist, the farmer, and they'll also be how you can upgrade your gear so your weapons and armor can keep up in the tough areas. Make sure they finally set up shop, survivors will need a bed and some kind of shelter. Your first villager will likely be the blacksmith, but if you follow the main quest line, it'll naturally lead you to most survivors. Though there are some out there that you'll just have to find on your own. Tip five is about food, comfort, and buffs. Food in Enshrouded really isn't for healing, it's mainly for the buffs. Each food type boosts a different stat. For example, meat will boost your health, veggies will help dex builds, grains will help strength, and mushrooms will help magic. Baseline, you'll start with three food slots, and you can only have one of each type active at the same time. So build your meals around your classes and keep a few on hand before any big trips. And then we have comfort. As you fill up your base with furniture, lights, and other decorations, your comfort level will go up. And when you rest at your base, the higher your comfort, the longer the rested buff will last, which gives you more stamina while you're out exploring. So decorating isn't really cosmetic, it actually makes you stronger. There are a few other little buffs as well. Rune shrines will give you a buff if you visit them, and if you have a cat or dog at your base, give them a pet before you head out for another small buff. Bonus tip, don't forget to pay that flame's tax, y'all. One like or one sub really helps channel out and I appreciate the support. Tip six is about important skills and respecializing. As you level up, you'll earn skill points and while there are a lot of options, there are a few skills I recommend grabbing early no matter what build you're going for. Double jump is a must. It makes getting around and dodging in combat a whole lot easier. Updraft is another one giving your glider a boost upward so you can travel a little bit further. Quick note though, in 1.0 you can actually choose between the newer or older version of updraft so pick whichever one you prefer. I'd also recommend desert stomach for an extra food slot. Also, right after that is last meal which keeps your food buffs even when you die, but they only last half as long. So that one's more of a personal preference. For combat, my personal favorites are kick and blink, but feel free to play around with all of your skill points. If you don't like your build, reskilling is super cheap. Just head to your flame altar and spend some runes to get all of your skill points back. If you don't know, you can get runes by salvaging weapons and magical items you don't need. Since it's so easy to reset, don't stress about getting your build perfect the first time. Tip seven is about ancient spires. Every time you unlock a new zone, I always recommend heading straight to the ancient spire first, which is that giant tower up in the sky. Completing a spire will give you a free fast travel point and since spires are some of the tallest structures in the game, they're also perfect launching spots for your glider. Once you've gone through the portal at the bottom and completed the trial, you'll be able to fast travel at the top anytime, jump off and glide towards your next destination, which makes exploring a new zone a whole lot easier. Plus the views up there are honestly some of the best in the game. Tip number eight is about shroud roots. Shroud roots are essentially what are keeping the shroud alive and you'll find them deep inside shroud covered areas, many times at the bottom of elixir wells. Chopping down a shroud root with your axe will clear a big pocket of the shroud and it'll also give you extra skill points, which is huge for your build. So if you see one, I definitely recommend going after it. Just make sure you're prepared and also keep an eye on your shroud timer, maybe bring some food and potions, and be ready fight because many times they're usually being guarded. Tip nine is about farming and animals. Farming is not as complicated as it may seem at first. You'll be able to craft seed beds and farm soil, turn the plants that you find into seeds, and grow your own crops instead of foraging everything. Your crops will also keep growing while you're off doing other things. So, when you come back home, you'll have ingredients ready to go. Most plants can just grow in the dirt, but shoot for soil if you can find it. Farm soil is a lot better than nothing, but if you want optimal growth, definitely get the fertilized farm soil. Also, a little tip about managing your farms, if you pull out your watering can and look at the plants, it will actually give you a drop down on how the plant is doing and what it needs. So, this tool alone will be a huge help when you start planning your farms. On top of that, you can tame animals, and once you feed them and give them a place to live, they'll produce resources like milk, eggs, and wool. If you want to tame animals, you're going to need some bait, which you can get from the farmer. She will also give you a little farming quest, which will act like a little taming tutorial. Essentially, what you're going to need to do is sneak up on the animal and throw bait near it, then pet the animal repeatedly while it's distracted. Also, if you happen to have two animals of the same species and at least one spare bed, there is a chance each night they could have some babies. The catch here is that dogs and cats don't produce resources, and farm animals can be killed by hostile enemies. So, be sure to keep them protected. And tip 10 is about the Hollow Halls. These are large expansive dungeons that you'll find all over the map, and most of them require a key to get in. The first Hollow Halls won't require a key, but you'll be put on a quest to go there from your alchemist Balthazar. Once inside, you'll work your way through rooms full of enemies and puzzles until you reach the boss at the end. These dungeons can be pretty long, so make sure to bring plenty of food, potions, and consumables before going in. Lastly, before you leave that first Springlands Hollow Halls, make sure to pick up at least 50 ectoplasm fragments from inside the dungeon, because you're going to need it to craft your next key. Each zone will have its own Hollow Halls, and completing it will reward you with good gear, unique loot, and resources that you can't really get anywhere else. If you're doing this co-op or with a group of other players, think about composition synergy. Bring a front liner, damage dealers, healers, and it'll start feeling like a good old MMO dungeon run with your buddies.
