# Does Enshrouded 1.0 Live Up to the Expectations?
# Louey7 | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C-ahOSLWnd8 | sous-titres: en-orig

Enshrouded has left early access with its full 1.0 launch that we have been waiting almost 3 years for. Expectations were sky-high, but does 1.0 live up to those standards of being a fully finished, newly transformed game? Perhaps more importantly, is it worth your time and the money? I played early access for over a thousand hours, so it was hard to not notice some of the visual issues and performance struggles of the game. &gt;&gt; [music] &gt;&gt; I was instantly amazed at how much smoother the game ran while also looking better. The huge upgrade in the level of detail on terrain and blocks from not only far away, but even closer distances was the first thing [music] that I noticed. It looks good, especially in terms of some of the new lighting and shadow details, such as the sun rays going through the trees. These visual improvements are important though, because they are what set up moments like looking out at the Embervale after you exit the Cinder Vault. &gt;&gt; The world is appealing. I know I cannot recreate that experience after having played early access, but it is hard to deny the sights that catch the eye. It's part of why the handcrafted, manually built world of Enshrouded is amazing. In experiencing this world, it is hard to overstate how important the optimization and performance improvements are. This had been one of the roughest parts of early access, and importantly, now it feels better. From the beginning in the Springlands to the new lava and floating islands region, Enshrouded looks good. Enshrouded feels good. All of that is meaningless without good progression that is core to survival games and RPGs. Overall, I love the progression of Enshrouded. It is exciting to reach new biomes, unlock new crafting recipes, discover new building blocks and terrain materials, and deepen your a [music] skills. With my fresh start play through for the early game, I can say that is solid, but it has some kinks. First, with early game resources, particularly plant fiber and shroud spores, it can be a struggle to get enough of these early on, especially if you were playing multiplayer and want to build a house right away. Fortunately, Enshrouded does offer robust difficulty settings, which I have a whole guide on over on my second channel, by the way. But on default, that pacing just felt a little bit off, especially if you were following the quests, which would be reasonable since Enshrouded is more quest driven than other survival games. Those aside, the bigger kink in progression actually has to do with weapons and armor. [music] You get all sorts of weapons and even armor pieces and rings early on from mob drops and chests. This is exciting and it is fun to try out different things. There is RNG in the drops, but it doesn't feel bad because then looting and upgrading is exciting. But early on, you only unlock one survivor, the blacksmith, and he can only upgrade specific armor rings and importantly weapons. So, if you want to upgrade a really cool wand or bow, for example, you are out of luck and just need to wait. The reason this feels off is because you can upgrade melee weapons and the heavier tankier armor and rings, but just not the dextrous or magic based armor and rings. The problem this creates is if you want to be using one of those other options, the game gives them to you to enjoy, but you can't progress them. So, it feels oddly unsupported. [music] That said, it is a bit of a tricky problem to solve because it is thematic for the upgrades to be tied to the survivor craftsperson that works with those [music] pieces of gear. Beyond those elements, though, the progression of early game works quite well overall. With all of the content that they have added since early access and in 1.0, there is a lot to discover, including on the edges of the world, which provide interesting new spots to build a base, as well. The new voice overs for the lore notes is really nice, as well. It's almost like having a super brief audio book excerpt play while you are playing, and it has increased my immersion with the story, which can be a challenging task to get me so immersed. Skipping way ahead, all the way to the newest biome added in 1.0, this is a quite interesting region that feels like a proper post-apocalyptic wasteland. It gives a sense of a more ultimate danger and like you are getting to the root of what has been going on throughout the rest of the world. I have not played a ton of the new content yet, but all of it has been really fun so far. [music] If you are returning player, you will notice that there is a lot of new throughout the rest of the world, but for jumping straight into the new biome, it works well. And I personally am super excited to get through all of the new content and see all of the new progression and, of course, the ultimate story conclusion. I look forward to discovering all of that and even what else is new in the world while playing my main mage character, which received a substantial overhaul in 1.0. Jumping in, the mage is fun to play. The wand abilities add a lot more diversity in the gameplay and increased engagement rather than just spam clicking, which is greatly appreciated. With the staff, you have a large variety of spells that have many different effects, which also makes for highly engaging gameplay. In comparison to before 1.0, this feels a whole lot better. It is still familiar, but the variety, the new effects, and just everything makes it feel transformed and a whole lot of fun. The progression has also been pretty nice. Right off the bat, there is more spell variety in the little gym. Mana drain is surprisingly helpful, since it gives you a way to regenerate your mana if you prefer to just keep your staff out and be a true staff-focused wizard. From there, it only gets better as you progress. I'm super excited to play more with all of the new spells and get the mid-game progression with those. While less major, I've also been impressed with the other archetypes. The new elemental eternal arrows are wonderful for archers, but I have not experienced the actual progression of them yet. Melee also feels solid. Perhaps the biggest improvement from their overhaul before the 1.0 launch is that heavy attacks are even more impactful, which is nice and further motivates changing up your attacks. For the other side of combat, I love the new enemy variety. If you are starting out fresh, there is a decent mix-up of early-game enemies, but from the perspective of someone with many early access hours, I like seeing the new enemies. It really does feel more varied throughout, and that is something I appreciate. The new guardian enemies in the Sinner's Cradle are also quite neat, and they fit in really well with the biome and new dungeons. The only real disappointment I had here was that the the Veilwater Scavengers are still dressed for the mountains rather than the jungle. Maybe they are just crazy, but that has felt off ever since they were added with the Veilwater Basin update, and I was admittedly hoping they would be fixed in 1.0. With combat, early-game balance is decent, but experiences can vary based on your exact gear setup and skills build. One thing I will say is that there are a few particularly strong skills in the skill tree early on that are like leagues above the rest, but after a few hours and a bit of progression, gaining skill points, you will be able to get deeper into your core archetype. Until that point though, it is quite difficult to not pick up the easy constitution nodes give you a substantial survivability boost. Fortunately, it is easy to respec later, but this left me with [music] a not fully satisfied and slightly odd feeling. More broadly, I love the ideas of the different skills, the amount of skill points you get, and flexibility to put them wherever you want, but I can't help but feel like this could still use some bigger improvements such as separating adventuring [music] utility and combat focus skills. On this note, one of the distinctly worst things about 1.0 is spending skill points and refunding them. It used to be quick and easy to spend points, move on, spend more points, or undo if you are refunding at the flame altar, but now there more menu clicks and it takes longer to spend points. [music] The UI is also a little bit rougher and feels less polished with all that. This might seem like a tiny deal, but I was admittedly disappointed with this experience. In a similar vein, some other UI elements with the inventory and tooltips also feel a bit worse. I suspect this is done to make it easier to see on console from far away, but it has made things a bit more difficult to manage overall. That said, there is a ton of quality of life that has been introduced in 1.0. For inventory management, the starter small chest and magic counterpart is so so much better than before. I absolutely love that you can [music] quickly get small magic chests blown up, especially since those are needed to make crafting and even building a whole lot easier. This was a small and unannounced change, but it makes a huge impact to the new player experience that is worth much more than a small chest worth of praise. Auto loot is also nice and is especially funny if you have it always on as rocks [music] and branches follow you around the Ember Vale. That quick aside, this really has been quite nice, especially in chopping trees and breaking many of the loot storing decorations throughout the world. Enemy loot could still be made much faster, especially now that the pacing really doesn't line up with the rest of looting. Hopefully, that is the type of thing that the developers will continue to work on after this 1.0 launch. On a more positive note, being able to name map markers has also been an instant upgrade. I know my wife has already made use of naming some of her favorite or deposits to mine. The new realistic character proportions are also a really nice option if you like that. All of these quality of life additions have made the gameplay, progression, and general feel of the world more enjoyable. But, what about back at home? A huge part of Enshrouded is in its robust building system. If you are playing with a group and plan to build an Enshrouded, then having a dedicated server can be a great way to always have access to your world. With my G Portal link, [music] you can get 10% off an official Enshrouded server. While there is not much technical purpose or even need for fancy buildings or forts, it is one of my favorite activities in the game. There's a lot that is immediately better in 1.0. We have already talked about general graphics, but there's new textures for things like the field stones and the plant fiber roofs and much more that just really help with the visuals and feel of building. And that is all nice, but what's even bigger is the new 1.0 features. Being able to get rid of the shroud within your flame alter area is huge. I haven't had a chance to play around with this a ton, but I am looking forward to it. There are so many new building possibilities enabled by this, even daisy chaining flame alters to build in some really crazy areas that were completely unimaginable before. For more of the day-to-day building projects, another huge addition is flying. At least functionally [music] free flying for building. This makes it easier and safer to build in general, but it will also be really helpful in building floating islands or buildings or things like towers, which I plan to build plenty of. The future of building is bright, [music] and I am really excited to renovate our village and take on new projects. For the spark work system, that is definitely intriguing, but I have not unlocked it yet and have not started with the system. So, I will point you to the chat with my wife for more of my anticipation and expectations [music] on that. Bringing all of this together though, I ask the question of if Enshrouded feels like a true full release game and if it is worth it. If I do compare to other non-early access games, Enshrouded offers a ton of content, a solid progression loop, some wonderful gameplay, and my favorite building system of any game. With the quality of life, performance, and visual improvements, those help cement Enshrouded 1.0 as feeling like a proper complete game based on my initial 1.0 experience. But, there are still areas where the game could be refined and I will further explore the worthiness of Enshrouded in a future video. But, so far it is hard to say anything other than yes, Enshrouded is worth it. At least given that you enjoy its mixture [music] of survival, RPG, and building.
