Hi, I'm Annf and today I'm going to give you guys a quick little rundown of Enshrouded if you've never played before and you're looking to play for the 1.0 release. This is a beginner's guide and I've avoided spoilers as much as possible. This is just some tips and tricks for beginners before you start. First, we'll go over the settings. There are some preset options if you can't be bothered to go through the long list of settings you [music] can change. There's relaxed, default, hard, or survival, which is obviously the most difficult. There are also a ton of customization options if you want to change some settings. You can customize the difficulty settings for enemies, bosses, how frequently you'll see enemies. You can customize your own stats like health, stamina, how much experience you'll receive from quests and exploring. You can change whether or not you'll starve to death if you don't eat. Change how long night lasts or whether you lose loot when you die. If you don't want your weapons to break, you can turn off weapon durability. And if you hate spending too much time gathering resources, you can change the drop rates, too. Settings can be changed from super relaxed gameplay to really difficult. Pick whatever you think suits your style most. Next is crafting and base tips. You can craft a few items in your inventory crafting when you start like axes, torches, and so on. But to build an actual base, you'll need to claim a piece of land using a flame alter. This unlocks a small piece of land to build it. The game will lead you to a suggested starting location, which is a great spot since there's a well nearby for drinking water and all the starting resources you'll need. Crafting a base in Enshrouded is pretty detailed. In your base, you'll be able to make crafting [music] benches that will unlock lots of new recipes. NPCs you come across via quests will unlock more benches for crafting, and each will give you certain types of crafting paths. Be sure to look over everything you can craft when you discover new recipes. To craft a base, you need a construction hammer, and you'll need to craft necessary building blocks in the workbench before you start building anything. Once that's done, you can start building your base. So, you'll equip the construction hammer and press tab. Select the pieces you want to build and start placing them around. If you're like me with no building skills, you can make a nice little square big enough to fit a bed, fireplace, workbench, and a few chests for items you loot. There's also an undo option for convenience if you made a recent mistake with your placement. The game gives you all the information you need when you equip the construction hammer, so just check what options you have. If you're not a huge fan of spending hours building, there are also some pre-built towns not too far away from the starting area, which have wells, too, and sometimes farms. These can also be good spots to consider for your early bases. I've highlighted two on the map for you. Your flame altars can be upgraded for bigger building zones as you unlock more crafting options. You'll definitely need more space as time goes. To do this early in the game, you need to fight certain bosses to get shroud spores. You'll find these guys as you explore the shroud. They're obviously a little tougher than regular mobs, so make sure you're prepared when you find them. Make sure you have arrows, bandages, and your weapons repaired, and so on. Flame alters can also be leveled up to increase the amount of time you can spend in the shroud, as well as used to reset your skill points. To reset your skill points, you need to use runes, which are gathered from chests, some fell enemies in the shroud, and salvaging gear and weapons you don't need. Always be sure to salvage all the extra gear and weapons you find for more runes. They're useful for plenty more than just skill resets. Your base has a comfort stat, which extends the length of the rested bonus you get at the base. The rested bonus gives you increased stamina and stamina regeneration, so it's really helpful to have when you explore. Raising that stat lets you adventure longer. Even in the early game, it pays to build things that add comfort, like lights, beds, decorations, and so on. Check the comfort tab on your build menus to find these items. You only get a bonus for one item from each category, so two chairs are no better than one chair. Every comfort level raises your rested bonus by 1 minute. If you run out of the rested buff while you explore, you can get a small amount back by digging a hole in the ground or side of a cliff. Then use a fireplace or throw a torch on the ground by pressing E when you have it equipped. You can also use the torch to remove the wet debuff after the rain ends. Now we'll touch on quests and exploration. You can press J to open your quest log. Here you'll see different types of quests like main story quests and side quests. Most of the quests should show up around the map as icons determined by the type of category they're in. There aren't always markers on the map for important points of interest, so like in a lot of survival crafting games, remember to mark points of interests on the map yourself. Lore items exist around the world, so explore every building and dungeon you find thoroughly. You'll often find things like books that will give you hints to where a treasure might be located, so be sure to read and put the clues together to find the treasure nearby. While you explore, break down boxes and barrels you see for extra materials. And keep an eye out for things you can gather while you explore, as some things might blend in well with your surroundings. Essentially, loot everything and interact with everything you can. It can be helpful to keep one extra altar unused as a return spot for places you explore. If you need to go back to base and drop off items, get food, rest, etc., you can easily return to where you were last and destroy the alter to continue on. Try to unlock the grappling hook and glider as quickly as possible, as this will help you explore so much more quickly, and a lot of exploration requires them to access. These are easy to craft early on. A brief run through the shroud and farming mobs in the starting area will get you all you need. Unlocking new survivors is one of the most important keys to progressing in Enred, as they give you new crafting recipes, upgrades for gear, and weapons depending on their specialization, and workstations. So, be sure to prioritize these quests when you see them. One of the perks of the blacksmith, the first survivor you rescue, for example, is the small magic chest that lets you access the items from within the chest from a workbench for crafting. Anytime you see a new ancient spire that you can access, be sure to go through its puzzles to unlock a new fast travel point. These are great for exploration since you can jump off and glide to a new destination and opening up the map further for traveling from point to point. Be sure to keep an eye on your timer when you're in the shroud. The moment the timer goes down to zero, you'll instantly die. Always try to keep your escape route through the shroud in mind. The end of the timer creeps up on you fast. If you're struggling with the shroud, locate shroud roots in game to take out the area the shroud is encompassing so you can explore safely. These do respawn after a time, but it can still help with exploration. You can also reset your shroud timer if you find a beacon inside the shroud and click on it. If you're exploring and see a deadly shroud alert, this means you need to level up your flame alter further before exploring these areas. These zones are normally a slight pinkish color, so you should be able to tell before you enter them. Leveling up your altar will also increase the time you can spend in the shroud. Food buffs are pretty simple. By default, you can have three food buffs going at once, which you can see by pressing N and checking the status details at the bottom right. There are different food buff categories in game like meats, water, fruits, etc. You can only have one of each category active at a time. Cooked food will always give you a higher bonus than raw foods. Just be careful not to burn the food. For water, craft a water pouch and fill it at the well nearby or at a lake. So, last we'll go over skills. I'm definitely not an expert for skill paths, and these really depend on gameplay preferences, like what weapons you want to use and what gear you're using. But these are just some useful skill tree features to consider as you go. When going through the skill tree, consider choosing skills that will help empower your combat style as well as what you enjoy for exploration. Respacking is thankfully not very expensive and in shrouded, so if you want to try out new weapons or skills, don't hesitate. I'd really recommend getting double jump immediately. It's super useful to get height for gliding and accessing certain paths around the map. Updraft is another skill that lets you boost up once while gliding. Airborne reduces the glider stamina cost by 12%. So, if you plan to explore using the glider a lot, jumping from mountains or spires, this can be really useful. If you find you struggle with surviving in fights, the constitution skills will increase your HP. Some skills help with resource gathering, like minor. So, if you find it slow to gather certain resources, you can select these skills. Well, rested is a skill that will add extra time to your rested bonus to help you explore for longer without losing your buff. Late into the skill tree, there's a skill called dessert stomach. This gives you an additional food buff, so you can have four buffs instead of three. And last, there's two skills called inner fires and relentless flame that will increase the time you can spend in the shroud before the timer runs out. Just make sure you read through all the skills available as you go to help decide what path you want to go on for your playthrough.
