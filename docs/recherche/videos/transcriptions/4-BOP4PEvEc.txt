# Enshrouded 1.0 Will Blow Your Mind
# Veteran Joystick | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4-BOP4PEvEc | sous-titres: en-orig

Enshrouded 1.0 is a game I never want to end. It was already something special in 2024 at its early access launch, but now it's gone way beyond anything I ever expected. Because I hadn't touched this game in almost 3 years. I played it back then, loved it, then moved on and left it to cook knowing I'd be back eventually for 1.0. So when the Enshrouded team reached out to me to sponsor this video, I didn't exactly need much convincing. So going back into Enshrouded for the first time in 3 years, I expected just more of something I already loved. But what I found was something far bigger and more impressive than I ever imagined. And after spending close to 100 hours with 1.0 now over the last 2 weeks, spending what felt like literally every waking moment living inside this world, I'm still desperate to go back. Let me show you why. So if you haven't played it before, Enshrouded is a solo or co-op survival action RPG from Keen Games set in the enormous hand-crafted fantasy world of Embervale. Embervale itself was once a thriving kingdom, then a mysterious substance called the Elixir was discovered. Brewed from fungal roots deep underground, granting incredible abilities to anyone who used it. But as humanity dug deeper to extract it, the true cost revealed itself. Those roots released spores into the air forming a dense corrupting fog known as the shroud, and anything it touches is changed forever. Infested, absorbed, and destroyed. The people grew addicted, the kingdom fractured, and years of blight and war left Embervale in ruins. So united by desperation, the survivors of an ancient civilization and what remained of humanity created one being capable of surviving inside the shroud itself, the Flameborn. That's you, and you've just woken up into a dying world and you're essentially its last hope. The only one who can survive the shroud, uncover what really happened to the ancient, and reclaim Embervale. And the world you're doing all of that in is big. I mean, stupidly big. Seven completely different biomes now, deserts, frozen mountain peaks, overgrown ruins, and literal islands in the sky. Countless quests, places to explore, dungeons to find, bosses to hunt. I mean, pretty much anywhere you can see, you can go. And there's always something there to find. But it's not just big, it's ridiculously deep, too. You're building entire settlements from nothing, scavenging supplies, rescuing survivors who unlock new crafting possibilities, raising animals, farming, fishing, cooking, and a million things I've probably forgotten to include. But it isn't just busy work for the sake of it. All of it feeds into the same thing. Exploring further, getting stronger, surviving longer, and pushing deeper into this world and uncovering its secrets. Most survival games give you tons of freedom, but no real reason to use it. And so many RPGs give you a great story and just kind of funnel you through it. Enshrouded gives you both, and that's what makes it feel so different. It doesn't feel like a survival game, it doesn't feel like an RPG, it feels like an actual adventure. And that's before we even get to what 1.0 brings. Because this isn't just the end of early access, this is now the game Keen say they wanted to make from the beginning. The story's complete, the entire map's unlocked, including the game's final biome, the final piece of this already huge world, the new volcanic region, Cinders Cradle. But honestly, calling it just a new biome is kind of underselling it, because it's closer to two. Because as you progress through it, you'll eventually unlock the Sky Islands, and this place is just nuts. An entire end-game zone built in the sky. I mean, just look at it. The problem I had, though, was actually getting there in the first place, because Enshrouded is dangerously good at distracting you, making you just want to be in this world, constantly giving you something to work towards, giving you a glimpse of something in the distance that completely derails any plans you might have had, including just logging off and going to sleep. And this slapped me across the face big time in the very first biome, because at around 25 hours into this playthrough, I realized I'd seen maybe 1% of the game. Not because I was stuck, I just kept finding things I genuinely wanted to do. I'd spot something in the distance, go and investigate it, find a quest, get distracted by a ruin, realize I needed a specific resource, then end up somewhere completely different having forgotten the reason I went out in the first place. And somewhere in the middle of all of that, I ended up breeding goats for their milk. Because what Enshrouded does brilliantly is make you just want to be in this world, want to see what's around the corner, burning hours upon hours, not really making progress, just enjoying being there. But the thing is, even when I wasn't progressing the main story, I never felt like I was wasting time or missing out on the actual game. Whether I was exploring, getting lost in the shroud, chasing some random quest, picking mushrooms, or just messing about with my settlement, it all felt worthwhile. It all fed back into the overall progression of my character and preparing for my next adventure. Until eventually, I had to force myself to stop and start progressing through the biomes, not because I was bored or I'd hit a wall, quite the opposite, but because I had to make this bloody video and I couldn't afford to spend another 50 hours in the Springlands. Left to my own devices, I'd probably still be there now. But when I finally did drag myself away from the Springlands, the problem only got worse. I had almost 3 years of Enshrouded to catch up on, eight major updates across early access to get through, whole new regions, dungeons, bosses, quests, rare fish to catch, and fighting an unhealthy addiction to just jump off every cliff and glide across the sky. And normally, this is where a game this size starts to lose me a bit. There's only so many quests, crafting materials, and unexplored corners of a map I can stare at before it starts feeling less like an adventure and more like a to-do list standing in the way of all of the good stuff. But within Enshrouded, it was the complete opposite. The more I played, the more I wanted to keep playing. I wasn't just dragging myself through the game just to see what 1.0 had added. I was genuinely excited to see all of it. And man, it was one hell of an adventure getting there. Take Veil water basin. This entire region dropped during early access alongside the addition of proper water physics, swimming, and fishing. And I know getting this excited about water in a video game sounds silly, but honestly, this place is just beautiful. I mean, look at it. Then you've got the Alchemist Summit biome where you're exploring these enormous frozen mountains, dealing with the cold, trying not to freeze to death, and eventually fighting a bloody dragon on top of a mountain. Or the Hollow Halls. These things are enormous. Proper sprawling dungeons packed with enemies, getting swarmed by what felt like a hundred skeletons, completely underprepared with only one bandage to keep me alive. There were so many moments like this that I was just completely blown away. And the mad part is I'd still barely even touched the main event of 1.0. So when I did finally get to Cinders Cradle, I would have been absolutely fine with more of the same. I'd already seen so much. But Keen clearly weren't finished because 1.0 adds this enormous end-game volcanic region, an entirely new faction of ancient constructs, a new end-game boss, and that's alongside two more enormous Hollow Hall dungeons, new spells, overhauled magic combat, a higher level cap, another crafting tier, and a major graphical overhaul across the entire game. Plus those ridiculous sky islands waiting above it all. And that's what blows my mind because after close to a hundred hours in Enshrouded, there's still so much I haven't seen, so much I haven't even touched, so much more that I want to do. Keen have somehow crammed an absolutely ridiculous amount into this game, and none of it feels like it's there to just pad things out. I genuinely want to experience all of it, and if that means sacrificing sleep for the next 6 months to do it, then so be it. My goats aren't going to raise themselves. But, that's just what Enshrouded is today. The potential for what it could become next is somehow even more ridiculous because Keen haven't just created this enormous world for us to explore. They've literally given us the tools to build entire worlds inside it. Earlier this year, they dropped adventure sharing, basically allowing you to share your worlds with other players. And some of the stuff people have already built is insane. I've been to the Shire, Rivendell, Dracula's castle, entire towns and settlements that must have taken thousands of hours to build. But, now with 1.0, Keen are taking that a step further with something called Spark Works. Essentially, now we have the tools to make those same creations interactive. Add switches, pressure plates, traps, logic systems, even enemy encounters that trigger based on what you do. And they've already demonstrated what's possible. An entire custom dungeon with spike traps, skeleton ambushes, jumping puzzles, and even a room that literally starts filling with water while you're trapped inside it. And just think about what that could mean because suddenly these incredible castles and settlements aren't just somewhere to visit. They could become entire adventures for other players to experience. And considering what the community has already managed to build without it, imagine where this could go next. We could reach a point where Enshrouded is as much about the adventures its players create as it is about the one Keen built in the first place. And I can't wait to see it, which feels pretty fitting actually because for almost 3 years during early access, the people playing Enshrouded have helped shape what it's become. And for me, it's the perfect example of early access done right because Keen had a clear vision for the game they wanted to make, but they listened to the people actually playing it along the way. Roadmap shaped by player feedback, eight major updates, and a willingness to keep going back and improving what they'd already built rather than just more content for the sake of it. And just look at what it's become. Enshrouded in 2024 was great, but Enshrouded today, to me, is a genuine masterpiece. And those 100 hours were only the beginning. So, if you've been waiting for 1.0, I really don't think you'll be disappointed at all. And if you've never played it before, you've got one hell of an adventure ahead of you. Go check it out. I can't recommend it enough.
