The first mistake most new builders make is spending forever with a pickaxe. Just trying to level a patch of ground. It's slow, it's messy, and it never looks perfectly flat. The secret? Use the construction hammer itself as an excavation tool. Instead of chipping away, open your menu, grab the biggest foundation block you have, like the 4 m block, and just place it down on uneven ground, and then simply remove it. When you pull up a building block, it takes all the terrain inside with it, leaving a perfectly flat, perfectly square hole. You can repeat this to carve out a massive level area for your fortress in seconds, not hours. It's the fastest way to prep for a build site, and the game never really mentions it. All right, so our area is level. The next trap people fall into is laying down a single flat layer for the foundation. It works for sure, but it's why so many builds looks like simple boxes. The secret to a base that looks epic is creating depth from the very first block. Instead of one layer, think of two. First, lay down the main foundation. Then, switch to a smaller block size, maybe a 2 m block, and then lay a second layer on top, but pull it in one block from the edge. This instantly creates a border, a frame if you will, for your whole structure. It's a simple move that adds a professional level of detail, and it immediately kills that flat blocky look. Okay, this next one, total game changer for your walls. Because flat walls are boring walls, to fix this, we're going to use what I call the double wall illusion. Instead of one line of walls, you build two separate walls with a one block gap between them. It sounds like it uses more resources, but the payoff is huge. This technique gives you incredible architecture depth. It lets you build deeply recessed windows that look fortified in a more realistic way. You can do this with wall corners along the floor, but most importantly along the edges of your roof.
